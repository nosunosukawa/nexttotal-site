Road to the 1000 lb Club with 5x5: the best-case timeline

Updated October 16, 2026 · Numbers come from the same progression engine that runs inside the Next Total app

If every set goes as planned, a lifter with a 700 lb total reaches 1000 lb in about 8 weeks of 5x5, three days a week. Real life adds stalls, so treat this as the floor, not the promise.

The best-case table

Each row starts from an estimated total split 35% squat, 25% bench, 40% deadlift, training three days a week. "Sessions" counts workouts until the estimated squat + bench + deadlift reaches 1000 lb, assuming every work set is completed.

This is a calculation where every set succeeds. Most lifters stall well before the table says, especially past an 800 lb total. The useful part is the shape: how the 1000 lb gets split, and which lift has the most room left.

How the progression works

  • Squat and bench are 5 sets of 5. Deadlift is 1 set of 5.
  • Finish every rep and the next session adds 5 lb (deadlift adds 10 lb while it is light and has never stalled).
  • Miss reps and you repeat the weight. Miss the same weight three times and it drops by 10% so you can build back up.
  • Stall twice on 5x5 and that lift moves to a weekly cycle (heavy, light, medium days) instead of adding weight every session.

Why the split matters

A 1000 lb total is not three equal lifts. A common balance is about 350 squat, 250 bench and 400 deadlift. When one lift is far behind its share, that is usually the cheapest place to find the next 50 lb.
